# Customer-Churn-Analysis-Excel

# Project Overview

This project analyzes customer churn data to uncover insights about why customers leave and how churn rates vary across demographics and usage patterns. The goal is to create a clear, interactive Excel dashboard that helps identify churn drivers and provide data-driven insights.

This case study was completed as part of a guided project on DataCamp, but the analysis and dashboard design are my own work.

# Dashboard Overview

![Image](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/2fe7255d-aedd-4178-b521-7f359672ed34)

The dashboard provides an executive-level summary of customer churn, combining key KPIs, churn reasons, demographic insights, data usage patterns, and geographic analysis. It highlights where churn is most severe and points to customer segments and regions that require targeted retention strategies.

## 6. Churn Rate by Data Consumption

<img width="337" height="172" alt="Image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/ce33164d-1aad-4177-85f6-17ec5faf6faf" />


Churn is inversely related to data usage. Customers using 10GB or more have the lowest churn rate (27.72%), while churn rates are significantly higher among light users (less than 5GB: 34.69%) and mid-range users (5–10GB: 33.59%). This indicates that heavy data users are more loyal, possibly due to their higher dependency on services, while lighter users may perceive less value and switch more easily.

## 7. Churn Rate by State

# Executive Summary

The churn analysis reveals that nearly 27% of the customer base has been lost, posing a significant risk to revenue and long-term growth.

Churn drivers are multifaceted: competitor offers are the single biggest factor, but poor service experiences and device upgrade motivations also weigh heavily.
Age dynamics show that younger customers (19–28) are the least likely to churn, while older customers (79–88) face the highest churn risk at 43.8%.
Data usage patterns reveal that heavy users (10GB+) are more loyal, while light and mid-range users churn at higher rates.
Geographic variation highlights states like California, Indiana, and New Hampshire as churn hotspots, while states like Wisconsin and North Dakota remain relatively stable.

These insights suggest that retention efforts must address competitive pressure, customer service quality, device upgrade programs, and targeted interventions for vulnerable demographics and regions.

# Recommendations

## 1. Strengthen Competitive Positioning
   
  Counter competitor offers with more attractive bundles, device financing options, and targeted win-back campaigns.

## 2. Improve Customer Experience

   Train and support frontline staff to address service-attitude issues.
    Monitor CSAT/NPS scores and resolve escalations quickly.

## 3. Age-Specific Retention Strategies
  
  For older customers (79–88), provide dedicated support, personalized plans, or loyalty perks to address higher churn risk.

 ## 4.  Enhance Value for Light and Mid-Range Users

 Offer affordable entry-level bundles, loyalty rewards, or incentives to reduce churn among low-usage customers.

## 5. Geographic Targeting
   Deploy localized offers and service improvements in high-churn states like California, Indiana, and New Hampshire.
    Maintain and reinforce strong customer relationships in low-churn states.
